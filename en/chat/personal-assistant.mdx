The **Personal Assistant** is your private helper inside iTeam. Unlike the company agents (which are shared) and the [iTeam Assistant](/en/chat/iteam-assistant) (which helps you build the platform), the Personal Assistant is **yours alone**: there's **one per person**, it keeps **your** memory and works within **your** permissions in the company.

## What it is (and what makes it special)

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="One per person" icon="user">
    Each teammate has their own. It's not shared — it's your individual copilot.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Truly private" icon="lock">
    Only the owner chats with it. **Not even administrators** enter your conversation or read your personal memory.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Personal memory" icon="brain">
    It remembers how you work, your preferences and what you've asked before — so it doesn't start from scratch every time.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Knows the company (within your permissions)" icon="building">
    It sees the company's knowledge and resources **as far as your permission reaches** — never beyond.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## What to use it for

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Answer questions about the company" icon="circle-question">
    Ask about policies, processes and company information. It answers based on the knowledge **you** have access to.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Organize your day" icon="calendar-check">
    With accounts connected, it can look at your calendar, summarize emails and help you prioritize — like a personal executive assistant.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Remember your context" icon="bookmark">
    It keeps what matters to you (the projects you're on, how you like to receive answers) and uses it in later conversations.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Connect your accounts

You can ask the Personal Assistant to **connect your accounts** and, from then on, it helps with your email, calendar and messages.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask for the connection">
    Say, for example: *"connect my Gmail"*, *"link my Google Calendar"* or *"connect my Slack"*.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Authorize securely">
    Authorization happens through the provider's secure flow (login in the account itself). The Assistant **doesn't** ask for or store your password.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use it day-to-day">
    Once connected, ask things like *"summarize today's emails"* or *"what do I have on my calendar tomorrow?"*.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
The connected accounts are **yours**. They're tied to your Personal Assistant and serve to help you — they aren't shared with the company.
</Info>

<Warning>
Never paste passwords, keys or tokens directly into the conversation. Always use the secure connection flow (login at the provider). See best practices in [Security & Access](/en/administration/security-and-access).
</Warning>

## Privacy and isolation

<Info>
The Personal Assistant is **isolated**: your conversation and your personal memory belong to you alone. **Neither the owner nor the administrators** of the company can enter your conversation to read what you said — this is guaranteed by the platform, it's not just a setting.
</Info>

## Recovery when someone leaves the company

What if someone leaves the company? So important work information isn't lost, the company **owners** can **recover** the Personal Assistant of a teammate who left — in an **audited** way.

<Steps>
  <Step title="The teammate leaves the company">
    The person's access is ended normally.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The owner requests the recovery">
    Only the owner can initiate the recovery of that person's Personal Assistant, and the request is **logged**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Everything stays audited">
    The recovery produces a record (who, when, what). It's a controlled exception — not free access to people's privacy.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
While the person is at the company, privacy is total. Recovery exists only for the case of departure, and always with an audit trail — balancing individual privacy with continuity of work.
</Tip>
