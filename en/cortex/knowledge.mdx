**Knowledge** is your company's base of documentation and definitions, inside Cortex. Instead of knowledge being scattered across drives, PDFs and people's heads, you keep it here — and the **agents start answering based on it**, with a link to the source.

## How it works, in one sentence

You write **Pages**, add **documents** and **glossary terms** → iTeam **indexes** the content by meaning → when an agent (or Cortex) needs it, it **searches, uses and cites** it — respecting the **permissions** of whoever asked.

## Three things you keep here

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Pages" icon="file-pen">
    Your company's **living documentation**: policies, playbooks, procedures. Full editor (headings, lists, tables, images, callout panels), sub-pages, version history and **AI writing alongside you**.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Documents and files" icon="file-lines">
    What already exists: PDF, spreadsheet, deck, pasted text. You **upload the file** and iTeam extracts the content.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Glossary" icon="book-open">
    Your company's/industry's **terms** and acronyms, with definitions. E.g. in iGaming: *GGR, NGR, FTD, MTD*. That's how the agent speaks your language.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Pages: living documentation

A **Page** is the document your company reads every day — the refund policy, the onboarding walkthrough, the sales team rules. Click **New page** and start writing.

  <img src="/images/placeholder.svg" alt="Page editor" />
</Frame>

### Every page has an address

A page is not a dialog: it's a **screen with a link**. The address shows up in the browser bar — you can send it over chat, paste it in meeting notes, bookmark it, open it in another tab. The reading screen gives you **back**, a **clickable trail** up to the parent page, an **outline that jumps straight to a section**, sub-pages and related pages as links, a **copy link** button, the history, and **Edit** (which edits at the same address). Documents, files and glossary terms have their own screen too.

### The editor, for non-technical people

- **Plain-language formatting**: bold, italic, headings, lists, task lists, quote, code, divider, link.
- **Images**: drag them in, **paste** (Ctrl+V) straight from a screenshot, and **drag the edge to resize**.
- **Tables** with header row, add/remove row and column.
- **Callout panels** (tip, info, warning, success, error) for what must not be missed.
- **Automatic outline** built from the headings — the page navigates itself.
- **`/` menu**: type `/` mid-text and pick a block (heading, table, image, panel, list…).
- **Copy and paste from outside** keeps the styling (bold, lists, links, tables).
- **Local draft** on every keystroke: if the browser closes, your text is there when you come back.

<Tip>
A page only enters search and agent answers once it is **Published**. While it's a **draft**, it's yours to work on in peace.
</Tip>

### AI writes with you — and you say what you want

The **AI** button at the top of the editor opens the writing panel. The important part: there's a field where **you say what you want**, in plain language — *"focus on the sales team, include a table of minimum office days and an equipment checklist"* — and the AI follows it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Outline">
    Give the subject and the instruction. The AI proposes a **title + sections**, already formatted and consistent with what's already documented in your company (it reads Knowledge before writing).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Section by section">
    Ask for **one section** at a time ("Office days", "Exceptions"). The AI writes just that block and drops it in place — you review and move on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Improve what's already written">
    Select a passage and ask to **improve, shorten, expand, or turn it into a list/table**, with whatever tone instruction you want.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
The AI uses the **platform model** and never publishes for you: the text lands in the editor as a proposal — you review and publish.
</Info>

### Organization: tree, sub-pages and labels

- **Sub-pages** (up to 4 levels): click **+ sub-page** in the tree. Great for "Sales handbook → Prospecting → Contact cadence".
- **Drag to move** a page to another parent or another position.
- **Labels** (e.g. `policy`, `onboarding`) to filter the tree.
- **Page mentions**: type `@` and the title to create a **link between pages**. Each page shows **who cites it**.

### History and periodic review

Every publish creates a **version** with author and date. You open the **history**, compare and **restore** any earlier version — nothing is lost.

You can also set **"review every N days"**. Once the deadline passes, the page is flagged as **may be out of date** and the owner is **chased automatically** until they click *reviewed*.

<Warning>
The "may be out of date" flag is visible to readers and is taken into account by the agents: they warn you the source is overdue instead of answering as if it were fresh.
</Warning>

### On mobile

On the phone the page fills the screen below the app bar, with the formatting bar scrolling horizontally, the AI panel rising as a bottom sheet, and the same **save draft**, **publish**, **history** and **access** buttons.

## Adding a document or a file

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick the area">
    Every item belongs to an **area** (e.g. Marketing, Finance). That organizes the base and controls who sees it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload or write">
    **Upload a file** (iTeam extracts the text) **or** write straight in the editor. Give it a clear title — that's what search leans on later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set who can read it">
    Company, area, chosen people, or only you (see below).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
On save the content is **indexed** (turned into a representation the AI searches by meaning). Large items may take a few moments to become 100% searchable — the title is available immediately.
</Info>

## Who can read it: the four options

The **access** button in the page editor (and in the document form) offers exactly the same four options for every kind of content:

| Option | Who reads |
| --- | --- |
| **Whole company** | Any member — and the agents use it in anyone's answers. |
| **A single area** | Whoever belongs to the chosen area. Owner and admin can see it, for governance. |
| **Chosen people** | Only the people you tick in the list (plus the author). |
| **Only me** | Nobody else reads it — **not even an admin**, and not other people's agents. |

<Warning>
The rule covers **reading**, **search** and the **agents** — it is enforced inside the search mechanism itself, not just "hidden in the UI". What you restrict here does not show up in the answers of anyone without access.
</Warning>

## How the agents use it

When you ask an agent (or **Cortex**, or your **Personal Assistant**) something, it **searches Knowledge** and answers with the relevant passages, citing the title. When the source is a **Page**, it hands you the page **link** — one click and you're in the document.

The agent can also **propose documentation**: ask *"document this as a page"* and it creates the page as a **draft**, with the link, for a human to review and publish.

<Tip>
Start with your **industry glossary** and **2 or 3 key pages** (support policy, business rules, one playbook). The payoff shows up in the very first conversation with an agent.
</Tip>

## Search by meaning

Search is **semantic**: you don't have to guess the exact word. Asking *"what's our discount limit?"* finds the right passage even if the page says *"commercial concessions policy"*. The **type** filter includes **Page**, alongside documents, files and glossary.

## Same thing in a project — and isolated

Every project has its own **Project Cortex** with its own Pages tab. It works the same way, but the content is **isolated**: a project page is only read by project members and only used by that project's agent.
